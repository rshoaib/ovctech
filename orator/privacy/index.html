Orator — Privacy Policy

Published by Rizwan Shoaib

Last updated: September 7, 2026

The short version

Orator watches you through the front camera and listens through the microphone while you rehearse. Neither the video nor the audio is ever recorded, saved, or uploaded. Both are analysed live on your phone and thrown away frame by frame. There are no accounts, no sign-in, and no server of ours. The app is free and supported by ads, and it uses Google's analytics and crash reporting — so it is not a zero-data app, and the sections below say exactly what those two things involve.

Camera and microphone — what actually happens

While a rehearsal is running, Orator uses the front camera to measure your head angle and expression, and the microphone to measure your pace, volume and filler words. Both streams are processed as they arrive and discarded immediately.

  • No video file and no audio file is ever written. The app has no recording feature, no gallery, and no export. There is nothing on your phone for it to upload later, because nothing was saved in the first place.
  • Speech is transcribed on your device only. Orator requires Android's on-device speech recogniser and will not fall back to a network one. If your phone has no offline language pack installed, the app tells you so and the speech features stay switched off — it does not quietly send your rehearsal to a server instead.
  • The transcript never leaves your phone. It exists only in memory during the session, so the app can count filler words and words per minute, and it is gone when the session ends.
  • Your session scores are held in the app's own private storage on your device. There is no backend to send them to, no account to attach them to, and no sync.

Data we do NOT collect

  • ❌ We do NOT ask for your name, email address, or phone number
  • ❌ We do NOT require an account or login
  • ❌ We do NOT record, store, or upload video or audio
  • ❌ We do NOT upload your transcript, your scores, or your session history
  • ❌ We do NOT collect your precise location
  • ❌ We do NOT access your contacts, photos, or files
  • ❌ We do NOT sell your personal data

Advertising

Orator is free and supported by ads served by Google AdMob. Ads never appear during a rehearsal. To serve them, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects limited advertising data, which may include your device's advertising ID, app interactions, diagnostics, and an approximate location inferred from your IP address. Google is an advertising partner rather than a processor acting only for us, so this data is both collected and shared, and it is used for analytics, advertising and fraud prevention.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, the app shows you Google's consent form the first time it runs and honours your choice. If you do not consent to personalised ads, the app requests non-personalised ads instead. You can reset your device advertising ID or opt out of ad personalisation at any time in your Android settings, under Settings → Privacy → Ads.

Google's handling of this data is described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Analytics and crash reporting

Orator uses Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics to understand whether the app works in the wild and to find crashes. Here Google acts as a service provider on our behalf, so this data is collected but not shared onward.

The app sends exactly two events, and their contents are deliberately narrow:

  • A rehearsal finished — how many seconds it lasted, and whether the camera and the microphone each produced any data at all. Not your scores, not your transcript, and nothing about what you said.
  • Speech could not run — the reason it failed, for example that no offline language pack was installed. This is how we learn how often that happens.

Alongside those, Firebase collects the usual technical information: app version, device model, operating system version, a randomly generated installation identifier, an approximate location derived from your IP address, and crash reports with stack traces. This data is about the app, not about you personally, and it is not linked to a name or an email address because the app never asks for either. Google's practices are described in the Firebase Privacy and Security documentation.

Permissions

  • Camera — to measure your head angle and expression live. No image is kept.
  • Microphone — to measure pace, volume and filler words live. No audio is kept.
  • Internet — to request ads and send crash and analytics reports. The rehearsal analysis itself does not use it.
  • Vibrate — for haptic feedback.
  • Advertising ID — used by Google AdMob to serve ads, as described above.

Orator requests no other permissions. It cannot read your files, your photos, your contacts, or your precise location.

Age

Orator's Google Play listing targets an audience of 18 and over, and it is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 18. If you believe a child has used the app, email us at the address below and we will delete anything we hold.

Ad content is limited to Google's "G" (general audience) rating.

Your choices

  • Deny the camera or microphone permission — the app still runs, and tells you which measurements it cannot make.
  • Turn off ad personalisation, or reset your advertising ID, in your Android settings.
  • Uninstall the app — this deletes your session history and every other locally stored value.
  • Email us to ask what we hold about you, or to ask us to delete it.

Not a medical or assessment tool

Orator is a practice aid. The scores it shows you are rough signals to rehearse against, not a measure of your ability, and they are not a diagnostic, screening, or clinical tool of any kind. It is not used to assess you for anyone else, and no score it produces is sent anywhere.
