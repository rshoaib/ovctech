📊 Futures Signals — Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 1, 2026

Introduction

Futures Signals ("we", "our", "the app") is a free crypto market-signal reader developed and published by Rizwan Shoaib. It reads public cryptocurrency market data for a fixed list of ten well-known perpetual futures pairs and derives trading levels from it using standard technical calculations. It has no accounts, no logins, no ads, and no analytics.

All of the analysis happens on a small server component (Google Cloud Functions and Firebase Firestore), not on your phone. The phone app is a viewer: it displays signals the server has already produced, and shows a live price row. This policy explains exactly what that server does and does not hold.

Data We Access

  • Public market data — the server, and the app's price row, connect to Binance's public, keyless market-data endpoints to read candlestick history and live prices for a fixed list of ten well-known trading pairs (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, HYPE, BNB, ZEC, DOGE, ADA and LINK against USDT). These are anonymous, read-only public price feeds. No API key, no account, and no personal or device identifier is sent to Binance. The app cannot place orders, cannot access any exchange account, and cannot move funds.
  • A notification token — only if you allow notifications — to send you a push notification when a signal is published or resolved, Android must give us a Firebase Cloud Messaging registration token for your installation. If, and only if, you grant the notification permission, that token is sent to our server and stored so a notification can be delivered to your device. If you deny or revoke the permission, no token is ever sent.

    The token is a random identifier for an app installation. It is not your name, email, phone number, advertising ID, or hardware ID; it changes if you reinstall the app, and it cannot be used to identify you personally. Alongside it we store only the platform ("android") and the app version, so a broken build can be recognised. Nothing about which pairs you look at, which signals you delete, or how you use the app is ever sent with it.

How Signals Are Produced

Signals are calculated from the public price history described above by applying published, standard technical-analysis calculations — candlestick reversal patterns, a 50-period moving-average trend filter, and a stop and target derived from the signalling candle's own high and low. There is no signal provider and no analyst.

The calculation runs entirely on our server, every five minutes, so that signals are produced whether the app is open, closed, or the phone is asleep. The results are written to a database that the app reads. That database holds only market levels — a pair name, a direction, an entry, a stop, a target, and whether the trade later hit its stop or its target. It contains nothing about you, and it is identical for every user; there is no per-user content of any kind.

The app itself performs no analysis. If it cannot reach the server, it simply shows nothing new — it does not compute signals on your device.

Storage On Your Device

The app keeps no personal data on your device. It has no settings to save, no accounts, and no local database. What you see on screen is read live from the signal database each time you open it.

Standard operating-system caches (such as the notification token Android itself holds) are removed when you uninstall the app.

Permissions

  • Internet — Required. Used to read Binance's public market-data endpoints and to read published signals from our server. It is not used for advertising, analytics, or transmitting anything about you.
  • Notifications — Optional. Used only to alert you when a signal is published or resolved. Granting it is what causes a notification token to be sent to our server (see above). Denying or revoking it means no token is ever sent, and the app still works as a viewer.
  • Vibrate — Used only for the notification's vibration.

The app does not request or use an advertising ID.

Data Retention & Deletion

The only thing held on our server that relates to your device is the notification token, and only if you allowed notifications. It is kept for as long as it stays valid. When Google tells us a token is dead — which is what happens when you uninstall the app or revoke the notification permission — our server deletes it automatically on the next send.

How to delete your data:

  • Revoke the notification permission in Settings → Apps → Futures Signals → Notifications. The stored token stops being valid and is deleted automatically.
  • Uninstall the app. This causes the stored notification token to be deleted automatically, and removes everything the app held on your device.
  • You can also email us at segmentbi@gmail.com to request deletion of a token, and we will remove it.

The signal records themselves contain no personal data, so there is nothing personal in them to delete. You can still remove any signal from the list inside the app.
